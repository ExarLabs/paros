## Abstract
The essay argues that teams spend effort making meetings engaging and pay for it in decision quality. Citing the author's own survey of 40 teams, it links varied meeting formats with decisions being revisited later. It proposes one fixed agenda (what changed, what is blocked, what we decide, who does what by when) and moves novelty to separate workshops. Against the objection of boredom, it answers that short meetings do not get boring, so boredom signals length, not plainness.

## Section analysis

### 1. The problem
- **Guiding question:** what does format variety cost?
- **Key ideas:** effort spent on format is taken from decisions; teams with the most varied formats report the most revisited decisions.
- **Skeptical challenge:** a correlation from the author's own unpublished survey; teams with unstable work may vary formats and revisit decisions for the same underlying reason. [Inference]

### 2. The proposal
- **Guiding question:** what should a recurring meeting look like?
- **Key ideas:** one four-part agenda every time; novelty only in workshops.
- **Applications:** paste the four headings into the standing meeting note; move retros and brainstorms out of the weekly slot.
