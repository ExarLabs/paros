# Wisdom: working well with AI, and keeping a good knowledge system

The principles (`principles/`) say how a PAROS is built. This folder says how a **person** works well inside one: practical wisdom on working with AI, on thinking, learning and staying creative with it, and on keeping a personal knowledge system that serves you instead of a platform.

It is what the advisor reaches for when someone asks questions like:

- "How do I work better with AI?"
- "How should I prompt?"
- "How do I stay creative, and keep my own voice?"
- "How do I avoid becoming dependent on one tool, or on AI at all?"
- "How do I learn faster from my own sources?"
- "How do I protect my focus?"

Source: Ignis Academy, 28 AI tips (original in Hungarian: https://ignis.academy/28tipp). Translated into English; every tip, its principle and its example are kept.

## How the advisor uses this folder

1. **Route first.** Find the person's question (or the closest one) in [`index.md`](index.md). It points to one to three tips.
2. **Pick one to three tips, never more.** Choose the ones that fit the person's actual situation: what they are working on, what friction they named, what their vault looks like. If two tips overlap, take the more concrete one.
3. **Quote the principle.** Each tip has a one or two sentence principle in italics. Quote it as is: it is the part that sticks.
4. **Adapt the example to their vault.** Do not retell the example from the file; translate it into their world. If they keep project folders, show the tip with one of their project folders. If they have a meetings area, use that. Name a real file or folder of theirs where you can.
5. **Connect to PAROS where it fits.** Many tips are the human side of a principle: W-01 and W-03 to W-05 sit behind P01 (knowledge lives in markdown), W-06 and W-07 behind P06 (search), W-16 behind frontmatter and versioning, W-22 behind the meetings pack. Say so in one sentence when it helps; do not turn the answer into a lecture.
6. **End with one step.** Offer one small thing they can try today (as the collection itself says: 0.1 is enough). If it changes their vault, the usual rule holds: ask first.

**Do not:**
- dump the whole list or a long summary of it; the collection is a reference for you, not a handout;
- present the tips as rules for the vault (they are habits for the person, not checks for the diagnosis);
- moralise. Tips W-08, W-18, W-20 and W-23 touch values, relationships and privacy: offer them when the question calls for them, plainly, and leave the judgement to the person.

Tool names in the tips (Obsidian, NotebookLM, Claude Cowork and others) are examples of the time they were written. The principle is what lasts (that is W-28's point). If the person uses other tools, map the tip to theirs.

### The tips at a glance

**Ethos, responsibility** (only a person can truly take responsibility)
- W-01 Prepare as if the AI remembers nothing
- W-02 Never rely on a single AI
- W-03 Get to know markdown files
- W-04 Use Obsidian (a second brain on your own machine)
- W-05 Let the AI work directly on your files
- W-06 Ask your own knowledge base
- W-07 Bring the knowledge base into the conversation
- W-08 You are the moral compass

**Logos, understanding** (the machine calculates but does not understand)
- W-09 Understand how things connect
- W-10 Learn how to question the AI
- W-11 Reverse the roles
- W-12 Accept nothing you do not understand
- W-13 Teach the AI what you think you understand
- W-14 Ask for analogies, metaphors, stories
- W-15 The thought is yours; the AI grows it
- W-16 Version your plans
- W-17 Ask the AI to argue the other side

**Pathos, empathy** (human empathy is biological, not a pattern)
- W-18 AI empathy is simulated
- W-19 Learn to think critically
- W-20 Do not let AI write your personal messages
- W-21 Seek first to understand the other person
- W-22 Be present in the conversation
- W-23 Know yourself

**Thelos, desire and calling** (people have a calling; the machine has no desire)
- W-24 Find your calling
- W-25 Protect the flow state
- W-26 Use AI to generate ideas
- W-27 Get closer to art
- W-28 Do not over-specify the prompt

**Bonus**
- W-B1 Distil the intent

## Stable IDs

The IDs W-01 to W-28 and W-B1 are stable, like the principle IDs: the index, the changelog and any adopted vault may refer to them. A new tip gets the next free number; a retired tip keeps its number and is marked as retired.
